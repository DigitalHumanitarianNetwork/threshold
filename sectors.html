Where could this work for you?

Threshold Evaluations fits any field where a wrong AI answer can hurt someone and experts already know what good looks like. Find yours, and see what you could judge.

Law and justice

Law, in general

If you practise law, you could judge whether an AI answer states the rule correctly, cites the right source and flags where it depends on the jurisdiction.

Immigration law

If you advise people on visas or asylum claims, you could judge whether an answer gets deadlines, eligibility and evidence right.

Employment law

If you handle workplace disputes, you could judge answers on dismissal, pay and discrimination.

Housing and tenant law

If you help tenants, you could judge answers on evictions, repairs and deposits.

Contract law

If you draft or review contracts, you could judge whether an answer reads a clause the way a careful lawyer would.

Legal aid and law school clinics

If your clinic helps people who cannot pay a lawyer, you could judge the answers those people are most likely to read.

Rights, media and information

Human rights organisations

If you document abuses, you could judge answers on evidence, testimony and the legal standards that apply.

Journalism and fact-checking

If you run a newsroom or a verification desk, you could judge whether an answer is accurate, sourced and honest about what is not known.

Libraries, archives and heritage

If you look after collections, you could judge answers on provenance, cataloguing and how records are kept.

Translation and interpreting

If you work in minority or low-resource languages, you could judge whether an answer is faithful, natural and safe to rely on.

Health and care

Medicine and public health

If you are a clinician, nurse or community health worker, you could judge answers on diagnosis, dosing, triage and prevention.

Mental health and counselling

If you counsel people in distress, you could judge whether an answer is safe, kind and knows when to refer.

Social work and child protection

If you protect children and families, you could judge answers on risk, safeguarding duties and what to do next.

Accessibility and disability advocacy

If you advocate for disabled people, you could judge answers on rights, accommodations and access.

Education, work and livelihoods

Education

If you teach, tutor or support special needs, you could judge whether an answer is correct and pitched at the right level.

Insurance, claims and consumer protection

If you handle claims or advise consumers, you could judge answers on cover, exclusions and complaint routes.

Finance and tax help

If you work in microfinance, a credit union or free tax help, you could judge answers on loans, tax rules and fees.

Agriculture and food security

If you are an agronomist or work with a farmer cooperative, you could judge advice on crops, pests, soil and seasons.

Engineering and safety

If you work on buildings, water or sanitation, you could judge answers on codes, safe practice and failure risks.

Faith-based and community organisations

If you serve a congregation or a local community group, you could judge answers on the questions your members really ask.

Public sector

Asylum and migration agencies

If you work in a migration authority or on asylum cases, you could judge answers on procedure and protection rules.

Disaster response

If you work in emergency response, you could judge answers on needs, access and coordination.

Courts

If you work in the courts, you could judge answers on procedure, deadlines and what a court can and cannot do.

Public health agencies

If you work in a health agency, you could judge answers on surveillance, outbreaks and guidance to the public.
