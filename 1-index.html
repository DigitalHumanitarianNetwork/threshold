The problem

General benchmarks test general knowledge. They do not test yours.

AI models are being used for specialised work with no shared way to check whether they actually know the subject. A model that sounds confident can still be wrong about the things that matter in your field.

What it is

Your questions. Your standard. Their scores.

Threshold Evaluations is a service for testing AI models on the topics you know best.

Your questions

You build a set of questions on your topic, the way an expert would ask them.

Your standard

For each question you write the ideal answer: the key points a good answer must cover.

Their scores

The service asks every model your questions, checks each answer against your key points, and gives each model a score and a report.

How it works

Three steps, run as often as you need.

01

Ask

Your question set is sent to the models you choose, whenever you want: on a schedule, or when a new model comes out.

02

Score

Each answer is checked against your key points and scored from 0 to 1. A score of 0.7 or more is a pass.

03

Report

You get a per-model report: overall score, question by question, and how scores change over time.

Private by default

The test stays private. Publishing is your choice.

Your questions and ideal answers always stay private, so models cannot learn the test. The scores are yours: publish them for transparency, or keep them inside your organisation. Some industries will want a public record, others will not. Both work.

Who it is for

Humanitarian first.

Built for communities of practice. Any field where the quality of an answer matters, and where experts can say what good looks like.

Beyond humanitarian work

Any field with experts can set a standard.

Threshold works wherever a wrong AI answer can hurt someone and experts already know what a good answer looks like. Here are a few places to start.

Law, in general

If you practise law, you could judge whether an AI answer states the rule correctly, cites the right source and flags where it depends on the jurisdiction.

Human rights organisations

If you document abuses, you could judge answers on evidence, testimony and the legal standards that apply.

Journalism and fact-checking

If you run a newsroom or a verification desk, you could judge whether an answer is accurate, sourced and honest about what is not known.

Medicine and public health

If you are a clinician, nurse or community health worker, you could judge answers on diagnosis, dosing, triage and prevention.

Education

If you teach, tutor or support special needs, you could judge whether an answer is correct and pitched at the right level.

Insurance, claims and consumer protection

If you handle claims or advise consumers, you could judge answers on cover, exclusions and complaint routes.
